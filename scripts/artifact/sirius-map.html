sirius/v0.4.0/command map

Twenty commands, in the order you meet them

sirius finds money at risk in two different places, and almost every confusion about it comes from mixing them up. One half reads your code. The other half reads your operations — failed payments, abandoned checkouts, ageing invoices, books that disagree. Same binary, same shell, entirely different inputs.

Surface one

Money at risk in code

Scans a repository. Finds a hardcoded key, a SQL query built from a request parameter, a card number in a log — and prices each one in rupees against a PCI-DSS or DPDP clause.

scanfixtriageexplainreport

Surface two

Money at risk in operations

Reads a batch of records rather than a repository. Decides which failures are worth chasing, works them under a capacity cap, and refuses the ones a rule forbids. Everything simulated — there is no --execute.

revenuereconcile

If you run one thing

The sixty-second path

From a cold start in any repository. No config, no account, no server — the scan engine is compiled into the binary, so this works offline on the first try.

sirius scan . → sirius explain SIR-SEC-001 → sirius fix SIR-SEC-001 → sirius report
$ sirius scan . ✗ CRITICAL SIR-SEC-001 Hardcoded Stripe secret key src/config.py:14 · PCI-DSS 8.6.2 · RBI-DPSC · DPDP §8 ₹42,00,000 ↳ fix: env_lookup run sirius fix SIR-SEC-001 ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── Findings 2 critical 2 high 2 medium Money@risk ₹89,30,000 Compliance 60/100 Exit 1 severity≥high → BLOCKED

Two rupee figures, and they are not the same number. ₹42,00,000 is that one finding. ₹89,30,000 is all six together. Exit 1 means findings were found — it is the gate doing its job, not a crash.

Track 1

Is anything wrong with this code?

Where you start. Everything here runs with no backend at all.

/scan . → /triage decide what is real → /fix SIR-SEC-001 accept the diff
CommandWhat it doesWhat you get back
/scan/scan [path] [--json] [--sarif f] Walks a directory, parses each file into a syntax tree, runs 13 rules over it. Findings streaming as they are found, each with a clause and a rupee figure. Ends with totals and an exit code.
/explain/explain SIR-SEC-001 Shows how that rupee figure was derived — base amount, reachability, provider weight, age in version control. The whole derivation, so you can argue with it. Every figure states what it assumes.
/fix/fix <rule> [--all] [--apply] Proposes a patch through the Cerebus guardrail, then re-runs the rule to check the fix actually cleared it. A provenance panel, a diff, and a verifier verdict. Nothing is written until you accept.
/triage/triage [--severity level]full screen Walks the findings one at a time so you can accept, dismiss or suppress each. Decisions saved to .sirius/. Press q to come back to the shell.
/watch/watch [path]full screen Re-scans whenever a file changes. A live view. Ctrl-C returns you to the shell with the transcript intact.

Track 2

Money at risk in operations

A different input entirely: a batch of payment failures, abandoned checkouts and overdue invoices. gen makes a synthetic one so you can try the whole loop with nothing of your own at stake.

/revenue gen batch → /revenue detect batch → /revenue eval batch is it any good? → /revenue recover batch
SubcommandWhat it doesWhat you get back
revenue gen/revenue gen batch Generates a seeded batch of records with a known answer key, plus one gateway outage and one abuse ring. A folder of records and their ground truth, reproducible from the seed.
revenue detect/revenue detect batch Scores every record, names the outage, holds the ring for a human, and picks what to work under a capacity cap. Records streaming with a score and a reason, and a count of what it refuses to touch.
revenue eval/revenue eval batch Measures the detector against the half of the data it never saw. Precision, recall, what being wrong cost, calibration, and a comparison against sorting by amount.
revenue recover/revenue recover batch Works the queue under thirteen stopping rules — quiet hours, consent, contact limits, mandate caps. A timeline of actions taken and refused, money attributed honestly, and a signed hash-chained audit trail.
revenue stress/revenue stress Shifts the world the model was trained on — no outage, a mandate-heavy book, larger tickets — and measures what survives. Six worlds, how much edge each one costs, and whether a refit would recover it.
revenue sweep/revenue sweep --seeds 8 Runs the whole evaluation over N seeded batches, because one batch is an anecdote. Per-seed rows and a mean. --save / --against catch regressions.
revenue explain/revenue explain pay_00051 Why this one record scored what it did. The evidence ladder, feature by feature, and the rule that held it if one did.
revenue audit/revenue audit --verify <file> Checks the recovery trail has not been altered, deleted from, reordered or appended to. A verdict on the hash chain and the signature.
/reconcile/reconcile books [--gen] [--exceptions] Matches three sets of books — your ledger, the gateway's settlements, the bank statement — through five tiers of matching. Match rate, verified accuracy, and an honest list of what it could not resolve.

Track 3

Prove it to someone else

What you hand a reviewer, a pipeline, or an auditor.

/baseline set accept today's findings → /scan . only new ones appear → /report → /badge
CommandWhat it doesWhat you get back
/report/report [scan-id] [--format json] Builds a report from the last scan and signs it with an ed25519 key stored at 0600. A signed file. --verify gates on it and exits 0, 1 or 2.
/badge Writes an SVG of the compliance score, or prints the hosted URL when a project is configured. A badge for a README that only moves when someone scans and commits.
/baseline/baseline [set|show] Records today's findings as accepted, so later scans show only what is new. A stored fingerprint set in .sirius/. Totals respect it too.
/suppress/suppress <rule> --reason "…" Silences a rule — and requires a reason and an expiry date to do it. A suppression that stops counting in the totals, not just in the list.
/rules/rules [list|show|validate] [target] Lists the 13 compiled rules, shows one in detail, or validates a rule file against the conventions. Severity, category, clause numbers, fix action and the exact suppression comment to paste.

Track 4

When something looks wrong

Start with /doctor. It reports against the mode a scan will actually run in, which is usually the thing you are confused about.

CommandWhat it doesWhat you get back
/doctorno backend Self-tests both engines, names the working directory, checks the terminal's unicode and colour, and refuses a signing key that is not 0600. Which config layer every value came from — so "why did it do that?" has an answer.
/init Scaffolds sirius.yaml and .siriusignore, including the revenue policy thresholds. Config you can read and edit. Nothing hidden.
/login/login --api-key <key> Verifies a key, then stores it at 0600. Only needed for hosted history — scanning never requires it. A stored profile.
/logoutRemoves the stored profile.Back to local-only.
/cd/cd [path] Changes the directory everything runs in. Bare /cd tells you where you are. The new working directory, echoed in the header.
/help /clear /exit The shell itself. /help is this map in the terminal, grouped the same way. /clear returns to the opening screen, wordmark included.

Reading the result

What the exit code means

Modelled on Snyk. The one that surprises people is 1 — it means the scan worked and found something.

0
Clean
Nothing at or above your threshold.
1
Findings
Action needed — not an error. The gate worked.
2
Failure
Auth, network or a parse problem. Something broke.
3
No target
Nothing scannable was found at that path.

In a pipeline that should not block, the escape hatch is sirius scan . || true.

Before you file a bug

Four things that look broken and are not

Two different rupee figures on one screen

The first finding line carries its own amount. The footer totals every finding. They are supposed to differ — a single critical is not the whole repository.

The scan says Exit 1

That is a finding at or above your threshold, which is the tool doing what it was asked. Exit 2 is the one that means something went wrong.

/triage and /watch take the whole terminal

They draw their own full-screen interface, so the shell steps aside and hands them the real terminal — the way git hands over to your editor. q leaves triage, Ctrl-C leaves watch, and the transcript is still there when you come back.

Nothing in revenue contacts anybody

Every action is simulated and the output says so. There is deliberately no --execute flag; the audit trail records what would have happened, including everything a rule refused.

Every command works as sirius <name> from your shell, or /<name> inside sirius shell. The scan engine, the rules, the money model and the whole revenue surface run with no backend and no network.
